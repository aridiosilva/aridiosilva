My current work explores the convergence of **Agentic AI, Multi-Agent Systems, Edge AI, Zero-Trust Architecture, AI Security, Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC), and Security-by-Design**.

## Current Research

I am the creator and lead researcher of **SGAEIA — Secure Governed Autonomous Edge Intelligence Architecture**, an open and vendor-neutral reference architecture for governed distributed multi-agent systems.

SGAEIA investigates the transition from conventional Edge AI toward **Governed Autonomous Edge Intelligence**, where AI agents may perceive, reason, communicate, delegate, and act across heterogeneous edge-cloud infrastructures while remaining constrained by enforceable security and governance policies.

The architecture explores concepts including:

* bounded and revocable agent authority;
* authenticated and traceable delegation;
* Zero-Trust runtime enforcement;
* multi-agent trust boundaries;
* continuous Governance, Risk and Compliance;
* Security-by-Design, Security-First, and Shift-Left engineering;
* threat modeling using STRIDE and OWASP principles;
* formalized security invariants;
* Evidence-as-Code and continuous assurance;
* security-preserving technology substitution;
* distributed and hierarchical Edge AI architectures.

## Research Interests

My broader research interests include **Agentic AI Security, Multi-Agent Systems, Edge Intelligence, Distributed AI, Zero Trust, AI Governance, trustworthy autonomous systems, cyber-resilience, secure AI architectures, and continuous security assurance**.

I am especially interested in the architectural question:

> **How can autonomous AI systems be granted enough authority to perform useful actions while ensuring that such authority remains bounded, verifiable, auditable, and revocable?**

## Open Research

I believe that security and governance should be treated as architectural properties of autonomous AI systems rather than as controls added after deployment.

My work therefore follows an **open research and reproducible engineering approach**, combining conceptual architecture, formal specifications, threat models, security invariants, executable evidence, reference implementations, and persistent research artifacts.
